Failure-only precision recovery: arithmetic argument, validation and limits
2026-11-04; simplex2.0.22 implementation in ado/_dea_simplex.ado

Scope
This certifies enclosure of the screen's arithmetic within a deliberately restricted
binary64 domain, under round-to-nearest evaluation of the written elementary
operations. It is not a theorem that the screen proves exact LP optimality. Its
nonzero tolerance, finite empirical tests, scaling and feasible-point checks remain
separate issues. Public x is binary64; dual y is the sum of two stored binary64 values.
No directed hardware rounding or external arbitrary-precision runtime is required.

1. Error-free transforms
TwoSum(a,b) returns s,e with s+e=a+b exactly in real arithmetic. TwoProduct uses
Dekker splitting (2^27+1) and the parenthesized product remainder. We use the standard
identities described in Ogita/Rump/Oishi (2005), Algorithms 3.1–3.3 and Theorem 3.4.
https://www.tuhh.de/ti3/paper/rump/OgRuOi05.pdf
The code does not assume a fused multiply-add. Reassociation/fast-math would require
revalidation. The reference is used for these primitive identities, not as proof
that our complete LP policy is the same algorithm as the paper's Dot2Err.

Every nonzero product operand is restricted to [2^-400,2^400] in magnitude. Products
are bounded by 2^800, splitter intermediates by approximately 2^428; the smallest
possible nonzero product of significand pieces is at least 2^-904. These are normal
binary64 numbers. Zero products are exact and do not need reciprocal range shifts.
A dot is limited to 4096 terms and its initial D to the same operand envelope.
All sum-transform intermediates stay far below overflow and on a dyadic lattice no
finer than 2^-904. Out-of-envelope operations return missing (uncertain), never pass.
The existing simplex arithmetic and refinement solver are not redefined by this range.

2. Outward scalar operations
For a+b, TwoSum identifies the direction of rounding. If its remainder is zero or
already points inside the requested bound, the stored sum is the desired bound.
Otherwise add/subtract abs(s)*2^-52 in the required direction. For normal s this
increment is between one and two ulps of its binade, and nearest rounding moves by
at least one adjacent representable value. It therefore covers the remainder of a
nearest-rounded elementary operation. Boundary binades can only make the bound
more conservative. Negative s is handled using abs(s), with the requested sign.
The same construction applies to the high product using its exact product remainder.
No bump is applied when the elementary result is already exact.

The generic outward addition additionally rejects nonzero input magnitudes below
2^-970, inputs above 2^900, and nonzero results below 2^-970. The bump therefore
remains normal (at least 2^-1022); large intermediates cannot overflow. This is a
range guard, not a relaxation or rescaling of the user's tolerance. Constants are
exact binary64 literals, not Mata's approximate exponentiation.

3. Dot enclosure
Maintain h,l and nonnegative radius R for the partial exact value. For one product
p+e=a_i*b_i, perform these exact two-term transforms:
  s,s0 = TwoSum(h,-p)
  t,t0 = TwoSum(l,-e)
  u,u0 = TwoSum(s0,t)
  v,v0 = TwoSum(s,u)
The updated exact partial value equals v+v0+t0+u0 plus the prior unknown error.
Retain h=v,l=v0 and increase R by |t0|+|u0| using outward positive additions.
At completion TwoSum(h,l) gives center and a final remainder; add its magnitude to R.
Induction gives |(D-sum a_i*b_i)-center| <= R. The algorithm bounds actual discarded
remainders instead of using an arbitrary global multiplier on tolerance.

4. Propagation through the quality screen
Bound each original equality residual and each reduced cost using the dot enclosure.
For a value center +/- R, outward_add(|center|,R) bounds its absolute value, and
outward_add(center,-R,lower) is a lower bound. Consequently:
 - |y_h|+|y_l| is rounded upward;
 - the weighted primal sum uses upper absolute residuals, upward positive products,
   and upward sums;
 - the complementarity sum similarly bounds |x_j|*|reduced_cost_j|;
 - the dual violation is max(0,-lower_reduced_cost);
 - the gap is bounded directly from the combined c'x-y_h'b-y_l'b expression,
   avoiding cancellation of two separately rounded objective values.
Primal feasibility and nonnegative x remain independently checked by _dea_lpcheck.

5. Acceptance without a rounded denominator
Let O=(o,r) enclose c'x. Then |c'x| >= max(0,|o|-r).
Let N be an upper bound of one of the three nonnegative quality numerators.
If |o|>=r, evaluate and bound N-cutoff-cutoff*|o|+cutoff*r as a combined dot.
Otherwise evaluate N-cutoff. Accept this item only if its computed upper bound <=0.
This suffices for N <= cutoff*(1+|c'x|), while preserving the expression's low parts.
The diagnostic denlo and displayed normalized metrics are not used as a rounded
acceptance gate. This distinction resolves the three conservative false rejections
of the first prototype, which rounded the denominator downward too early.
The dual item similarly bounds violation-price*min(1,max|c|) as one expression.
Here cutoff=min(1e-8,tol), price=min(existing_opt_tol,tol), exactly as in T7.
Missing enclosures reject. The final acceptance also requires the unchanged primal
filter, valid basis, finite refined components and finite objective.

6. Packaged validation and cautions
Run test_precision_arithmetic.py with --stata and --output (a new directory).
It checks600 exact products,800 outward additions,108 dots up to4096 terms,
9 malformed/range controls,68 range-edge controls,420 quality cases,104 refinement
systems,21 cutoff neighbors,10 normal/error helper controls and4 unavailable
extreme scalar examples. The21 and420 boundary sets overlap and are not441
independent cases. The same input doubles are evaluated independently by Fraction.

Run test_precision_recovery.py with the same arguments and a separate new directory.
The hexadecimal fixture covers396 known-optimum LPs (180 square,216 with extra
columns or inactive inequalities). Exact primal/dual witnesses prove the optimum,
and279 formerly missing cases must return the exact point (114 original,153
transition, one certified-price and11 blocked-pivot reconstruction recoveries).
All396 conditions require availability. Other accepted cases
must satisfy their original primal tolerance and an independent objective benchmark.
One preexisting success is recorded separately: objective13510798723147367/2251799813685248
versus6, relative error1.0084892984199963e-8. This precise known observation is allowed
and reported; an improved value passing the benchmark is also allowed. New or larger
objective deviations fail the test. It is not counted as an accurate recovery.

Existing successful outputs are preserved. Whether to verify/refine all successful
results, or offer an optional diagnostic, is a separate policy question. Current
user tolerance is not a promise of relative objective error for every accepted LP.
The finite test sets and restricted arithmetic argument do not prove universal LP
optimality, recovery, portability or performance. Infeasible VRS super-efficiency,
artificial purge/rank, native IPM limitations and deferred inference work are separate.
All data and Python helpers needed by these tests are included in this package;
NumPy/SciPy and licensed Stata16+ are required. No private research paths or downloads.
